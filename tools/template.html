A Cross-Scenario Survey and Analysis of Self-Evolving Multimodal Agents

Jingjing Jiang

2026

Read the paper GitHub repository View framework Browse curated papers
Multimodal Reasoning Multimodal Generation GUI & Web Navigation Embodied Manipulation

Abstract

Recent work shows that multimodal agents increasingly self-improve without recurring human annotation. However, each self-improvement mechanism is designed and evaluated within a single scenario, leaving its preconditions unverified under cross-scenario transfer. This survey investigates the structural source of asymmetric mechanism transfer across four scenarios: multimodal reasoning, multimodal generation, GUI and Web navigation, and embodied manipulation.

We first formalize the self-evolving multimodal agent by four criteria and a differentia of self-reference, and adopt the three-stage self-evolving loop of experience generation, feedback acquisition, and update consolidation as our analytical framework to position the scenarios along ceilings on rollout count and feedback fidelity. Comparing self-evolving with fixed loops within each scenario separates the limitations that the scenario imposes from those inherent in the self-evolving design.

We then recast each mechanism as a trade-off that raises a scarce factor by presupposing a task-structural resource, such as output equivalence or checkable outcomes, and judge it portable, conditional, blocked, or redundant across scenarios. We attribute the asymmetry to divergent combinations of scarce factors and resources across opposite transfer directions, and identify the matched-budget measurements that could overturn these still untested verdicts.

What counts as a self-evolving multimodal agent

The surveyed object is defined through a genus and a differentia. The genus comprises autonomous learning loops that satisfy four criteria, and the differentia, self-reference, requires the agent to realize at least one component of its own loop.

C1AutonomyThe agent operates without human intervention across goals, actions, feedback, and updates after initialization.
C2FreshnessExperience is generated under the current policy.
C3DurabilityUpdates persist beyond the episode boundary.
C4RecurrenceThe loop iterates across cycles.

Fixed loops as controls
  • DefinitionLoops in the genus that lack self-reference are fixed loops, for instance reinforcement learning against pre-collected answer keys under a stationary sampler.
  • RoleThey serve as controls in every scenario, separating the constraints a scenario imposes from the intrinsic effects of self-evolution.

The three-stage self-evolving loop

Each cycle decomposes into three stages, and each stage's bottleneck is formalized through two governing factors. The decomposition follows the generate, evaluate, and update pattern of Expert Iteration and ReST, and the analysis adds the factorization, the ceiling axes, and the cross-cycle edges.

Stage 1 · Experience Generation (EG)

goal sampler σω and rollout
The goal sampler draws a batch of goals, and the current agent rolls out under each goal, producing the trajectory batch. EG bounds how fast and how broadly the loop can improve.

feedback function Vν
The feedback function maps the trajectory batch to scores, preferences, or critiques used for the update. FA is the only stage that carries information about the idealized task reward.

Stage 3 · Update Consolidation (UC)

evolution operator fζ
The evolution operator writes the scored batch into at least one persistent component, namely parameters, memory, tools, or instructions. UC determines whether updates persist and generalize.
Sζ retentionGζ generalization
Across cycles, the agent edge carries the updated agent into the next rollout and, when the evaluator reads the agent, into the next feedback stage. The goal edge carries the updated history into the goal sampler and opens only when the sampler is adaptive. Five degradation modes travel along these paths: compounding reward hacking, and distribution shift along the agent edge or the environment path; forgetting and plasticity loss as a moving goal distribution makes parameter writes interfere; and curriculum collapse and model collapse, which contract the support of EG. The agent-edge extension into UC surfaces through erroneous writes.

Cross-scenario positioning matrix

Cross-scenario positioning matrix. The axes are the scenario-fixed ceilings K̄ and F̄, both decreasing away from the origin. Each scenario sits at the inferred height of its ceilings, with shading deepening from MR (neither binding) to EM (both binding). The MG, GW, and EM cells denote open-ended prompts, sandboxed environments with state scripts, and physical deployment, and dashed arrows show the displacement of closed-form generation sub-families, of navigation on live websites, and of simulated manipulation.

Each scenario imposes ceilings on the rollouts affordable per cycle (K̄) and the feedback fidelity attainable by admissible evaluators (F̄), which span a two-dimensional positioning matrix. Methods choose operating points below these ceilings, and the scenario fixes only the ceilings.

Multimodal ReasoningBoth ceilings high. Rollouts are single decoding passes and answers admit exact match.
Multimodal GenerationLow F̄. Open-ended images and videos have no reference output.
GUI & Web NavigationLow K̄. Multi-step episodes pay environment latency at every step.
Embodied ManipulationBoth ceilings low on hardware, with simulation raising both.
The matrix identifies which ceilings bind in each scenario but alone cannot determine whether a given mechanism is portable. Quadrant placements are inferred from single-scenario evidence, and the backbone's pretraining coverage remains a confound.

Cross-scenario asymmetry

Each self-evolving mechanism is a trade-off, in which the agent raises a scarce factor by replacing a fixed component with its own records, proposals, judgments, or memory writes, at the price of presupposing a task-structural resource, such as output equivalence or checkable outcomes. The asymmetry is attributed to the different combinations of scarce factors and resources that the two directions of a scenario pair encounter.

portable the target holds the resource the mechanism presupposes
conditional the resource holds only on a sub-family or through an adaptation, or the verdict is capped by an unobservable outcome
blocked the mechanism judges or creates goals whose outcome is unobservable
redundant the factor the mechanism raises is not scarce in the target
